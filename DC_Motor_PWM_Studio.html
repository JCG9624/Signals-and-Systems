Carrier frequency versus motor-terminal frequency

Unipolar PWM

Both H-bridge legs operate at 20 kHz, using opposite reference levels. Odd carrier harmonics cancel across the load; the first nonzero AC component is at 40 kHz = 2fPWM.

ma = -0.41  ·  DA = (1 + ma)/2 = 29.5%  ·  DB = 70.5%  ·  V̄ = -4.92 V

Here fPWM always means the carrier frequency set in the microcontroller. The modulation factor ma = V̄/Vdc is signed; the physical leg duty cycles DA and DB lie between 0 and 1.

Leg A is high when the carrier is below ma; leg B is high when it is below −ma. The motor sees their difference. There are two motor-terminal pulses per carrier period. Their sign follows ma. Because the differential waveform repeats every half carrier period, its fundamental is 2fPWM and all odd carrier harmonics cancel.

The mathematical connection

Δ(s) = (Rtot + sLtot)(Js + b) + kekt
Hi(s) = (Js + b)/Δ(s)  ·  Hω(s) = kt/Δ(s)

For a leg with physical duty D, the signed cosine coefficient is An(D) = 2Vdc sin(πnD)/(πn), for n ≥ 1, using the selected carrier phase. In unipolar PWM, DB = 1 − DA, so An(DB) = (−1)n+1An(DA). The difference cancels odd orders and doubles the even-order coefficients.

In bipolar mode the leg voltages are complementary at each instant, so their AC coefficients have opposite signs. Both modes have the same mean terminal voltage for a fixed ma. A constant load torque changes only the DC operating point.
