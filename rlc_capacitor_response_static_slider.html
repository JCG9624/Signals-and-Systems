Sinusoidal steady state

yss(t) = M sin(ω₀t + φ)
M = |HC(jω₀)|; φ = arg HC(jω₀).

Switching transient (zero-state)

ytr(t) = −vC,ss(0) FV(t) − iL,ss(0) FI(t)
Cancels the two initial circuit states of the steady-state particular solution.

Initial-condition response (zero-input)

yIC(t) = VC(0−) FV(t) + iL(0−) FI(t)
FV = g₀; FI = g₁/C.
FV is the selected output per unit initial capacitor voltage; FI is the selected output per unit initial inductor current.

Total selected voltage

y(t) = yss(t) + ytr(t) + yIC(t)
vC(0⁺) = VC(0⁻).
g₀(0) = 1, ġ₀(0) = 0; g₁(0) = 0, ġ₁(0) = 1. Both solve g̈ + (R/L)ġ + g/(LC) = 0.

What to notice: all three outputs belong to the same circuit and share its two stored-energy states. Capacitor voltage gives a low-pass response, resistor voltage a band-pass response, and inductor voltage a high-pass response. The switching transient is part of the zero-state response; the initial-condition response comes from stored capacitor and inductor energy. Matching both initial states to steady state cancels the net transient for all three outputs. Their total voltages always satisfy vC + vR + vL = u.
