Circuit and input: the s-plane

The causal sinusoidal input has Laplace poles at ±jω₀. Move the input-frequency slider to see them move along the imaginary axis.

× Circuit poles ○ System zeros ◆ Input poles
Exact pole–zero cancellation at the notch. The two input poles coincide with the LC transfer-function zeros. For zero initial conditions, the sinusoidal terms cancel in Y(s): only decaying natural modes remain, with no sustained output sinusoid.
Step-input pole cancellation at s = 0. The selected output has a zero at the origin, so the pole of the unit-step transform 1/s is cancelled in the zero-state output. There is no persistent DC component; decaying natural modes remain.
System poles depend on R, L, C; zeros depend on the selected voltage. The input has poles at ±jω₀ for a causal sinusoid or at 0 for a causal step. Axis units: rad/s. Pole–zero cancellation applies to the zero-state transform; initial conditions may introduce additional natural transients.

Sinusoidal steady state

yss(t) = M sin(ω₀t + φ)
M = |HC(jω₀)|; φ = arg HC(jω₀).

Switching transient (zero-state)

ytr(t) = −vC,ss(0) FV(t) − iL,ss(0) FI(t)
Cancels the two initial circuit states of the steady-state particular solution.

Initial-condition response (zero-input)

yIC(t) = VC(0−) FV(t) + iL(0−) FI(t)
FV = g₀; FI = g₁/C.
FV is the selected output per unit initial capacitor voltage; FI is the selected output per unit initial inductor current.

Total selected voltage

y(t) = yss(t) + ytr(t) + yIC(t)
vC(0⁺) = VC(0⁻).
g₀(0) = 1, ġ₀(0) = 0; g₁(0) = 0, ġ₁(0) = 1. Both solve g̈ + (R/L)ġ + g/(LC) = 0.

What to notice: all six outputs belong to the same circuit and share its two stored-energy states. Capacitor voltage gives a low-pass response, resistor voltage a band-pass response, and inductor voltage a high-pass response. Combined element voltages produce additional finite zeros or an LC notch. The switching transient is part of the zero-state response; the initial-condition response comes from stored capacitor and inductor energy. Matching both initial states to the particular solution cancels the net transient for all six outputs. Their total voltages always satisfy vC + vR + vL = u.

Bode plots of the selected output

Vary R, L and C above, then choose any of the six outputs. The magnitude and phase plots show the exact sinusoidal frequency response of the circuit. Dashed vertical markers identify characteristic frequencies; the teal marker marks the chosen frequency.

Step-input experiment: Bode plots still describe the circuit's response to sinusoidal test signals. The frequency slider is a Bode probe, not a frequency of the step.

Frequency response is independent of initial conditions. Initial capacitor voltage and inductor current affect the transient in the Time response tab, but not these Bode plots.
